01 — The question

Two birds almost no one sees

The Luzon Buttonquail and the Luzon Rail are found nowhere else on Earth, and both are poorly documented and data deficient. Without knowing where they persist, it is hard to protect them.

I conceived the research concept and co-developed the proposal with the research team, securing funding through the Oriental Bird Club Conservation Grant. The project sets out to establish the status and distribution of both species in the mountains of Nueva Vizcaya.

Luzon Buttonquail

Turnix worcesteri

Philippine endemic // elusive and rarely recorded.

Luzon Rail

Lewinia mirifica

Philippine endemic // presence documented by this project.

02 — The approach

Five ways of looking for a bird that hides

A multidisciplinary team, combining field ecology with the knowledge of the people who live there. I established and coordinate the team, overseeing fieldwork and logistics.

  1. Transect surveysWalking set routes to record birds seen and heard.
  2. Camera trapsMotion-triggered cameras watching the ground where shy species move.
  3. Passive acoustic monitoringRecorders listening for calls around the clock.
  4. Mist-nettingSafe capture, identification and release.
  5. Community interviewsLocal ecological knowledge from the people who know the land best.

03 — So far

What the project has found

  • Luzon Rail documented. Field evidence confirms the species’ presence, adding to knowledge of its distribution and informing future conservation priorities.
  • Local partnerships built. Work with the Kalahan Educational Foundation, NTFP-EP Asia and the Municipality of Santa Fe supports research access, community engagement and locally supported conservation.
  • Findings shared. As first author of the collaborative project report, I coordinated presentations and outreach materials for local government and community stakeholders.

04 — Field report

Told by our partners

The first field survey ran from 14 to 23 July 2026 in the forests of Imugan and Bantinan, in Santa Fe, within the Kalanguya-Ikalahan Ancestral Domain. NTFP-EP Asia published a field report on the survey, written by Sunshine Telio, their Programme Officer for Community-based Conservation.

NTFP-EP Asia // Blog // 28 October 2026 Searching for Luzon’s Lost Birds: What the Forests of the Kalanguya-Ikalahan Ancestral Domain Revealed Read the full report

Highlights from the report

  • 99 bird species recorded across the two barangays.
  • Luzon Rail confirmed in Imugan from a bird found on 23 July 2026 and a 2025 photograph of a live juvenile from a community member — both verified by Philippine avian experts Desmond Allen and Dr. Jelaine Gan. The report notes that the findings do not establish the species’ abundance, distribution or population status.
  • Possible Luzon Buttonquail. A ground-dwelling bird in a Bantinan camera trap resembles the species, but its identity remains unconfirmed.
  • Bukidnon Woodcock. Two birds were captured on camera in Bantinan, possibly the first evidence of the species in Nueva Vizcaya since the late 1960s.

Summary of the NTFP-EP Asia report; please refer to the original for full detail. The survey team included six Filipino bird researchers, with Kenneth among them.
