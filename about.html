Curriculum vitae The journey

My research interests and experience focus on cetaceans and birds, with a broader interest in biodiversity conservation and the impacts of human activities on wildlife.

I graduated with a BSc (Hons) in Ecology and Conservation from Lancaster University in 2021, and I’m currently pursuing an MSc in Marine Mammal Science at the University of St Andrews.

A different kind of field training

My journey into research has been shaped by personal challenges and a strong commitment to biodiversity conservation. In 2018, while on a study abroad programme at Macquarie University in Australia, I suffered a rare spinal cord injury known as Surfer’s Myelopathy that left me paralysed from the waist down.

Despite initially being warned that I might never walk again, I undertook an extensive rehabilitation journey while continuing my university studies — balancing physical recovery with academic commitments, including completing my final year during the COVID-19 pandemic.

“The importance of pursuing meaningful work despite uncertainty.”

I remained determined to complete my degree and pursue a career in ecological research and fieldwork. The experience strengthened my resilience and shaped the way I approach research and conservation, instilling in me a deep appreciation for perseverance and adaptability.

Medical note

Surfer’s myelopathy

What it is
A rare, non-traumatic spinal cord injury, first reported in 2004 in novice surfers.
Cause
Prolonged back arching, thought to restrict blood flow to the spinal cord.
Signs
Back pain, then leg weakness, numbness or paralysis.
Outlook
Varies; rehabilitation can restore some or much of the movement.

General information, not medical advice.

Sea and forest

Since graduating, I have gained research experience across the Philippines, the UK and Europe, in marine and terrestrial ecosystems: cetacean research and monitoring, offshore environmental surveys, ornithological fieldwork, biodiversity monitoring and wildlife conservation.

In the Philippines, I have worked on research involving the endangered Irrawaddy dolphins, and I lead a conservation project investigating two poorly documented and data-deficient bird species, the Luzon Buttonquail and Luzon Rail.

Paying my own way

Alongside my academic and research pursuits, I’ve worked as a cleaner, barista, bartender and wildlife tour guide to support myself financially. Balancing these roles with my studies and conservation work has strengthened my independence, adaptability and determination to pursue a career in research.

What I’m working towards

I’m particularly interested in understanding the impacts of human activities on wildlife and developing practical, locally driven conservation solutions. I hope to contribute to strengthening marine and wildlife research capacity in the Philippines, bridging the gap between scientific research and local communities, and supporting the long-term conservation of the country’s unique biodiversity.
