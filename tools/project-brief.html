01 — The question

Two birds almost no one sees

The Luzon Buttonquail and the Luzon Rail are found nowhere else on Earth, and both are poorly documented and data deficient. Without knowing where they persist, it is hard to protect them.

The project sets out to establish the status and distribution of both species in the mountains of Nueva Vizcaya, working with the people who live there. Kenneth conceived the research concept and co-developed the proposal with the research team.

Luzon Buttonquail

Turnix worcesteri

Philippine endemic. Elusive and rarely recorded.

Luzon Rail

Lewinia mirifica

Philippine endemic. Presence documented by this project.

02 — The approach

Five ways of looking for a bird that hides

A multidisciplinary team combining field ecology with local knowledge, established and coordinated by Kenneth.

  1. Transect surveysWalking set routes to record birds seen and heard.
  2. Camera trapsMotion-triggered cameras watching the ground where shy species move.
  3. Passive acoustic monitoringRecorders listening for calls around the clock.
  4. Mist-nettingSafe capture, identification and release.
  5. Community interviewsLocal ecological knowledge from the people who know the land best.
03 — So far

What the project has found

  • Luzon Rail documented. Field evidence confirms the species’ presence, adding to knowledge of its distribution and informing future conservation priorities.
  • Local partnerships built. Work with partners supports research access, community engagement and locally supported conservation.
  • Findings shared. As first author of the collaborative project report, Kenneth coordinated presentations and outreach materials for local government and community stakeholders.
04 — Field report

  • 99 bird species recorded across the two barangays.
  • Luzon Rail confirmed in Imugan, verified by avian experts Desmond Allen and Dr. Jelaine Gan. The report notes that the findings do not establish abundance, distribution or population status.
  • Possible Luzon Buttonquail. A ground-dwelling bird in a Bantinan camera trap resembles the species; identity unconfirmed.
  • Bukidnon Woodcock. Two birds on camera in Bantinan, possibly the first evidence in Nueva Vizcaya since the late 1960s.
